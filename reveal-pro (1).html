Three metadata families

EXIF is written by the camera: time, device, exposure, GPS. XMP is written by editing software: creator, edit history, document identifiers. IPTC is written by people: captions, bylines, copyright, keywords.

Most viewers show only the first. The other two are usually where the interesting answers live.

What the thumbnail reveals

Cameras embed a small preview image inside the file. When a photograph is edited, that preview is often left untouched — so the embedded thumbnail can still show the original framing.

Reveal extracts it and compares its dimensions with the main image. A mismatch is a reliable sign of later modification.

Error level analysis

ELA re-saves the image at a known quality and maps the difference. Areas saved at a different quality — typical of pasted or retouched regions — appear brighter than their surroundings.

It is indicative, not proof: textures, noise and sharpening also produce bright areas. Read it alongside the metadata, never instead of it.

Reveal Pro — a single, self-contained HTML file. Works offline, including the map, which is drawn from embedded vector data with no tiles and no requests.
